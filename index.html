    What counts as middle housing?

    Washington law defines middle housing as buildings compatible in scale with single-family houses that hold two or more attached, stacked or clustered homes: duplexes, triplexes, fourplexes, fiveplexes, sixplexes, townhouses, stacked flats, courtyard apartments and cottage housing (RCW 36.70A.030). Accessory dwelling units are not on that list, so a house with an ADU is counted separately.

    Tick "Middle housing" to see all five of its types at once, or pick individual types in any combination.

    Building types here are worked out from permit descriptions, project names and units per building, so a few may be wrong. Project size counts every home in the project, across all its buildings.

    How the timeline and places work

    First filed is the date of the earliest city record for the project, including a pre-development meeting. The city's public permit map keeps about three years of records, so nothing here shows a filing before December 2023. A few projects known only from the city's monthly report have no filing date.

    Reached its current stage is when this site first saw the project at that stage. Dates from before November 2026 are estimates.

    Neighborhoods are the city's 29 neighborhood councils and three City Council districts, worked out from each project's map point. Where the list shows a more familiar name, like Kendall Yards, the filter still uses the official council.
